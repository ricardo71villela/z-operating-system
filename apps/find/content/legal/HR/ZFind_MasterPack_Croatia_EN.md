> **Methodological note — produced in English rather than Croatian.** Croatia's official language is Croatian; this guide is produced in English — one of the six languages already used across this series — rather than introducing Croatian as a separate production language, since Croatia's international property market (Istria, the Dalmatian coast, Split, Zadar) is overwhelmingly served in English by local agents, lawyers, and the tourism sector. Croatia was selected as an additional dynamic market because it is the EU's and Eurozone's newest member on several fronts (euro adopted 2023, Schengen 2023) and is undergoing **simultaneous, active reform** across annual property taxation (a genuinely new national tax introduced 2025), foreign-buyer reciprocity (future liberalisation must be based on enacted Croatian law, not OECD-accession expectations), and short-term rental regulation (the Ministry states the new platform registration-number obligation is planned for early 2027, with no such obligation yet in force at its 22 May 2026 notice).

## A. BUYING A PROPERTY

**Level: NATIONAL** (Croatian Civil Obligations Act, notarial practice, Land Registry — Zemljišna knjiga/Gruntovnica)

**Typical sequence:**
1. **OIB (Osobni Identifikacijski Broj):** the Croatian personal identification number, mandatory for **every** property buyer, resident or not, before signing any contract, registering ownership, or paying taxes. Obtained free of charge at any Tax Administration (Porezna Uprava) office with a passport, in about 15 minutes — this is consistently described as the essential first step, ideally arranged before house-hunting begins, since without it the transaction cannot proceed at all.
2. **Due diligence:** Land Registry (Gruntovnica/Zemljišna knjiga) check for ownership and encumbrances, conducted by a local lawyer.
3. **Pre-contract:** often signed with a deposit of roughly **10%**, before final approval steps (where applicable) are completed.
4. **Ministry of Justice approval (non-EU buyers only):** see Section G — non-EU buyers must wait for this approval during this phase, typically taking **2–6 months**.
5. **Final purchase contract (kupoprodajni ugovor) before a Croatian notary:** the notary verifies the identities of the parties and ensures legal compliance.
6. **Real Estate Transfer Tax (RETT):** where VAT does not apply, RETT is **3% of market value**. In the ordinary notarised process, transaction information is transmitted to the Tax Administration by the competent public body/notary; payment follows the official tax-assessment procedure. Do not assume a universal buyer self-filing/payment deadline from signing.
7. **Land Registry registration:** the purchase deed is only fully enforceable after official registration in the Land Registry (Zemljišna knjiga), which requires the notarized purchase contract, proof of identity including the OIB, and — for non-EU buyers — the ministerial consent decision, before the registry will record ownership.

**Remote/power-of-attorney purchases:** with a power of attorney signed at a notary in the buyer's home country (properly apostilled for non-EU jurisdictions), a Croatian lawyer can complete the entire transaction on the buyer's behalf — many cross-border buyers complete the OIB application, pre-contract, notary signing, and Land Registry filing entirely remotely.

**Acquisition-cost structure:** where VAT does **not** apply, Real Estate Transfer Tax (RETT) is 3% of market value. Where the acquisition is subject to VAT, RETT does not apply to the same transfer. Do not add 25% VAT mechanically on top of an advertised new-build consumer price without checking whether VAT is already included and how the seller invoices the transaction. Notary, legal, agency and registry costs remain transaction-specific.

**First-home support — official 2026 baseline:** qualifying **Croatian citizens under 45** acquiring/building their first qualifying residence under contracts/permits from 1 January 2025 onward may obtain qualifying **RETT reimbursement or 50% of qualifying VAT**, subject to residence, prior-ownership, price/area and other statutory conditions. This is **not a general foreign-buyer rebate** and must be checked for the specific applicant and property.

## B. SELLING A PROPERTY

**Level: NATIONAL**

The seller's lawyer verifies property legality, checks for outstanding debts or mortgages, and ensures transaction compliance; the notary certifies signatures and identities, but the purchase deed is only fully enforceable after Land Registry registration.

**Typical costs:** agent commission (commonly split with the buyer), legal fees, and the seller's own capital gains tax exposure where applicable (Section C). RETT is always the buyer's obligation.

## C. TAXATION OF CAPITAL GAINS — OFFICIAL 2026 BASELINE

**Level: NATIONAL**.

Official Croatian government guidance resolves the earlier source conflict for the ordinary private disposal case covered here:
- a disposal **within three years** of acquisition can trigger income tax on the real-estate disposal gain;
- disposal **after three years** is exempt on this basis, subject to the statutory exceptions/anti-avoidance rules;
- where taxable, the government guidance states a **25% rate** on the relevant gain basis;
- the taxpayer must file with the competent Tax Administration office within **8 days** of the disposal when the taxable rule applies.

The guide's prior “two-year exemption” and multi-rate uncertainty were therefore not acceptable as canonical statements.

**LEGAL_STATUS: CURRENT_OFFICIAL_BASELINE** for the ordinary individual case; companies, business/dealer activity, repeated disposals, gifts/inheritance and non-standard structures still require specialist review.

## D. TAXES ON ACQUISITION AND ON PROPERTY OWNERSHIP

**Level: NATIONAL / MUNICIPAL**.

**Real Estate Transfer Tax (RETT):** gov.hr states **3% of market value** when the acquisition is not subject to VAT. The usual notarised transaction information is transmitted to the Tax Administration by the competent public body/notary; special filing duties apply where the document was not notarised or otherwise transmitted. The tax is paid following the tax decision under the official procedure — do not summarize the regime as a universal “buyer files and pays within 30 days of signing”.

**VAT:** where VAT applies to the transaction, RETT does not apply to that same acquisition. Confirm whether the quoted new-build price includes VAT and the supplier's VAT status.

**First-home support — confirmed, not merely reported:** Croatia operates a tax-refund support measure for qualifying Croatian citizens **under 45** acquiring/building their first qualifying residence under contracts/permits from 1 January 2025 onward. It can refund qualifying RETT or **50% of qualifying VAT**, subject to residence, prior-ownership, price/area and other statutory conditions. This is not a general foreign-buyer rebate.

**Annual property tax:** a municipal-rate recurring tax applies under the post-2025 framework, with statutory exemptions including qualifying primary residence and other categories. The rate for the municipality/property must be checked directly.

**LEGAL_STATUS: PROPERTY_AND_BUYER_SPECIFIC_CONFIRMATION REQUIRED** for VAT/RETT classification, first-home eligibility and annual tax exemption.

## E. RESIDENTIAL TENANCIES

**Level: NATIONAL**

Rental income generated in Croatia is subject to local tax and must be declared, regardless of the landlord's residence or nationality (see Section D for the applicable rate range). Ordinary long-term residential tenancy law follows the general Croatian Civil Obligations Act framework for lease agreements — this guide's research did not surface a distinct, heavily-reformed long-term tenancy statute comparable to the short-term rental reforms detailed below; **confirm current landlord-tenant notice periods, deposit rules, and termination grounds directly with a Croatian lawyer**, as this area was not covered in comparable depth by the sources available for this guide relative to the short-term rental and tax topics.

## F. CO-OWNERSHIP OF MULTI-UNIT BUILDINGS — RECENTLY REFORMED, WITH DIRECT SHORT-TERM-RENTAL CONSEQUENCES

**Level: NATIONAL** (Act on Building Management and Maintenance, Official Gazette 152/2024)

**A recently reformed framework with an important 2026 practical consequence:** Croatia's co-ownership (suvlasništvo) framework for multi-apartment buildings was updated under the **Act on Building Management and Maintenance (NN 152/2024)**. The single most significant practical consequence for investors in this series' context: **short-term rental in a multi-apartment building now requires the consent of two-thirds (2/3) of co-owners, calculated by ownership share.**

**Interaction with short-term letting:** this co-owner consent requirement is a **separate and additional hurdle** to the national short-term-rental registration requirements described in the module below — exactly the same "two independent obstacles" pattern seen in several other jurisdictions in this series (Spain, the Netherlands, Belgium, Cyprus). A buyer intending to short-term-let an apartment in a co-owned building must satisfy **both** the building-level 2/3 consent **and** the national/hospitality-activity registration and licensing requirements — clearing one does not clear the other.

## G. FOREIGN BUYER — EVOLVING RECIPROCITY FRAMEWORK, WITH POTENTIAL OECD-DRIVEN LIBERALIZATION

**Level: NATIONAL**.

EU/EEA acquisition follows the applicable EU/internal-market framework, while third-country acquisition may require Ministry approval and reciprocity depending on nationality and property type. Agricultural/forest land and special categories can be subject to separate restrictions.

**Correction canónica:** do not present possible OECD accession as if it will automatically or imminently grant all OECD nationals EU-equivalent property rights. Any future liberalisation must be based on enacted Croatian law, not an accession expectation.

A Croatian company can create a different acquisition route, but that does not eliminate land-use, beneficial-ownership, tax or sector-specific restrictions and should not be advertised as an automatic “workaround”.

**No property Golden Visa:** ownership alone does not create a Croatian residence permit.

**LEGAL_STATUS: REQUIRES_NATIONALITY_AND_ASSET_SPECIFIC_CONFIRMATION** with the Ministry/notary/lawyer.

## H. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

1. **What is RETT on a non-VAT real-estate acquisition?** 3% of market value under official guidance.
2. **Do I also pay RETT where VAT applies?** Not on the same VAT-taxed acquisition.
3. **Is the private capital-gains holding exemption two years?** No; official gov.hr guidance uses **three years**.
4. **What rate does that guidance state when a private disposal within three years is taxable?** 25% on the relevant gain basis.
5. **What is the filing deadline in that case?** Eight days under the cited official guidance.
6. **Is there a first-home refund?** Yes for qualifying Croatian citizens under 45 and other statutory conditions; it is not a universal buyer relief.
7. **Can it include VAT?** The support can cover 50% of qualifying VAT under the official measure.
8. **Is annual property tax uniform nationally?** No; municipalities set the rate within the statutory framework.
9. **Do I need an OIB?** Yes, it is a core tax/identity requirement for property dealings.
10. **Are EU/EEA and third-country buyers treated identically?** No; third-country permit/reciprocity rules may apply.
11. **Will OECD membership automatically remove those rules?** Do not assume so; only enacted Croatian law controls.
12. **Is a national short-term registration number already mandatory in August 2026?** No. The Ministry stated on 22 May 2026 that **no such obligation currently exists**.
13. **When is the new number expected?** Beginning in 2027, exact date after enactment of the new Hospitality Act.
14. **Will it be digital/free?** The Ministry announced a free, digital process issued by the Ministry.
15. **Do building/co-owner rules still matter?** Yes, independently from national tourism registration.

## I. GLOSSARY

- **OIB (Osobni Identifikacijski Broj):** the mandatory Croatian personal identification number required for any property transaction.
- **Zemljišna knjiga / Gruntovnica:** the Croatian Land Registry, whose registration is the actual moment ownership becomes fully enforceable.
- **Kupoprodajni ugovor:** the final purchase contract signed before a Croatian notary.
- **RETT (Real Estate Transfer Tax):** the 3% transfer tax on resale property purchases, paid by the buyer.
- **Prirez:** the local municipal surtax applied on top of certain national taxes, including (where applicable) capital gains tax.
- **Reciprocity:** the principle governing whether a non-EU citizen may buy Croatian property, based on whether their home country extends equivalent rights to Croatian citizens.
- **D.o.o.:** the Croatian limited liability company structure, commonly used by foreign buyers (of any nationality) to acquire property, including land otherwise restricted to individual foreign ownership.
- **Act on Building Management and Maintenance (NN 152/2024):** the framework requiring two-thirds co-owner consent for short-term rental in multi-apartment buildings.
- **Iznajmljivač:** a registered private individual host/renter under Croatia's tourist accommodation framework.
- **Paušalni porez:** the flat-rate tourist-accommodation income tax, calculated per registered bed and set within nationally-bounded ranges by each municipality.
- **Hospitality Industry Act (Zakon o ugostiteljskoj djelatnosti):** the national law regulating tourist accommodation, including private short-term rental.

## SEPARATE MODULE: SHORT-TERM RENTAL — ONE OF THE MOST ACTIVE MARKETS IN EUROPE, ENTERING A NEW REGULATORY PHASE

**Current 2026 regime:** existing Croatian hospitality/tourism registration and local tax obligations remain applicable to hosts. Building/co-owner consent rules may impose an additional layer.

**EU Regulation 2024/1028:** the EU data framework is applicable, but Croatia's **new unique registration-number obligation was not yet in force in May/August 2026**.

**Official Ministry correction:** on **22 May 2026**, the Ministry of Tourism and Sport stated explicitly that **there is currently NO obligation to obtain the new registration number for platform advertising**. The obligation is planned to begin **in early 2027**, with the exact date to be announced after adoption of the new Hospitality Industry Act. The Ministry states the number will be free, digital and Ministry-issued.

**2026 Hospitality Act:** the Government adopted a **proposal** in June 2026. Until final enactment/effective dates are confirmed, draft provisions must not be described as operative host duties.

**Building layer:** consent/co-ownership requirements are separate and can apply irrespective of the future platform registration number.

**LEGAL_STATUS: CURRENT_OFFICIAL_TRANSITION** — existing host registration/tax/building obligations apply now; new EU-aligned registration number begins only when the Croatian implementing legislation makes it operative in 2027.
