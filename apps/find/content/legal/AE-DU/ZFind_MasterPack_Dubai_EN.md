> **Methodological note:** the UAE is a federation of seven emirates, and real estate law is **emirate-specific** — Dubai's framework (Dubai Land Department, RERA, DTCM/DET) does not automatically apply in Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, or elsewhere. This guide covers **Dubai only**. Dubai's property market operates under a structurally distinctive **zero-tax regime** relative to every other jurisdiction in this series — no personal income tax, no capital gains tax on property disposal, no annual property tax, and no tax on rental income — with government revenue instead concentrated in one-time transaction fees (principally the 4% DLD transfer fee). This, combined with 100% foreign freehold ownership in designated zones and a very fast, digitally-administered registration system, makes Dubai one of the most distinctive jurisdictions covered in this entire series.

## A. BUYING A PROPERTY

**Level: EMIRATE-WIDE** (Dubai Land Department — DLD; Real Estate Regulatory Agency — RERA)

**Freehold zones — the foundational legal concept:** foreign nationals of any nationality may own property with **full freehold title** only within **designated freehold zones**, established under **Law No. 7 of 2006** and **Regulation No. 3 of 2006**. Popular freehold areas include Downtown Dubai, Dubai Marina, Business Bay, Jumeirah Village Circle (JVC), Dubai Hills, Arjan, and Palm Jumeirah, among many others. Outside designated freehold zones, foreign ownership is generally not available in the same form. Dubai law also recognizes **usufruct and long-term leasehold rights up to 99 years**, though these are used far less frequently than freehold.

**No residency, visa, sponsor, or minimum age beyond 21 required to buy:** foreign buyers can purchase freehold property in Dubai while living abroad, visiting on a tourist visa, without a UAE residency visa, local sponsor, or local bank account — a structural contrast with several jurisdictions in this series that impose approval or residency-linked conditions on non-resident buyers. **The minimum buyer age is 21.**

**Typical sequence:**
1. **Verify freehold zone status and developer credentials** — ideally with a RERA-licensed broker.
2. **Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) / Sales and Purchase Agreement (SPA):** sets out price, payment terms, and conditions; a **holding deposit, typically 10%** of the purchase price, is paid at this stage.
3. **No Objection Certificate (NOC):** the developer issues an NOC confirming no outstanding obligations on the property (for off-plan, confirming the unit can be transferred); a financing bank issues its own NOC for mortgage buyers.
4. **DLD transfer appointment:** buyer and seller (or authorized representatives) attend together; the transfer fee is paid and the **title deed is issued in the buyer's name**.
5. **Off-plan properties:** initially registered under an **Oqood** certificate (an interim registration with the DLD) rather than a full title deed; upon project completion, the Oqood converts to a title deed for a further administrative fee.

**Timeline:** for a ready property, typically **2 to 6 weeks** from MoU signing to title deed issuance — notably faster than most jurisdictions in this series. Off-plan timing depends on the construction schedule. Once documentation is properly prepared, the DLD registration step itself often completes within hours.

**Costs — the 4% DLD transfer fee and related charges:**
- **DLD sale-registration fees:** the current Dubai Land Department service schedule states **Seller: 2% of the sale value; Buyer: 2% of the sale value**. Parties may negotiate the economic allocation between themselves, but the canonical legal/service-fee schedule must not be rewritten as “the buyer legally pays 4%”. Additional trustee/title/map/knowledge/innovation fees apply under the DLD schedule.
- **Trustee office / registration fee:** AED 2,000 (+5% VAT) for properties under AED 500,000, or AED 4,000 (+5% VAT) for properties at or above that threshold.
- **Title deed issuance fee:** AED 250 (some sources cite AED 580 for the standard administrative fee on a ready property, with lower fees for off-plan interim registration).
- **Map fee:** approximately AED 250.
- **Mortgage registration (if financing):** 0.25% of the loan amount, plus approximately AED 290.
- **Total closing costs:** commonly summarized as **6%–10%** on top of the purchase price once agent commission (typically 2%) and developer NOC charges (typically AED 500–5,000) are included.

**No age-related ownership restriction** beyond the 21-year minimum, subject to guardianship rules where relevant.

## B. SELLING A PROPERTY

**Level: EMIRATE-WIDE**

The seller obtains a developer NOC confirming no outstanding service charges or obligations, attends the DLD transfer appointment alongside the buyer, and (by market convention, though not strict legal default) commonly shares the 4% transfer fee, though in practice buyers typically absorb the full amount as described in Section A.

**Existing tenancy on sale:** if a landlord sells a property during an active tenancy, **the new owner must honor the existing lease agreement until it expires** — the tenancy is not automatically terminated by a change of ownership.

**No capital gains tax:** see Section C — the seller's proceeds are not subject to any Dubai or UAE capital gains tax.

## C. TAXATION — A STRUCTURALLY DISTINCTIVE ZERO-TAX REGIME

**Level: EMIRATE-WIDE / FEDERAL (UAE)**

**No property tax, no capital gains tax, no personal income tax, no tax on rental income.** This is one of the most structurally distinctive features among all jurisdictions covered in this series: Dubai property investors pay **no annual recurring property tax**, **no capital gains tax on disposal**, and **no personal income tax on rental income received** — the entire tax burden associated with property in Dubai is concentrated in the **one-time transaction fees** described in Section A (principally the 4% DLD transfer fee) plus ongoing **service charges** (Section F) and, for landlords letting short-term, the specific tourism-related charges described in the short-term rental module.

**Never assume** any capital-gains-style calculation, holding-period exemption, or annual wealth/property tax exists in Dubai comparable to virtually every other jurisdiction in this series — the absence of these taxes is the single most important structural fact for any comparison against European, American, or Latin American markets covered elsewhere in this guide series.

## D. TAXES ON ACQUISITION AND ON PROPERTY OWNERSHIP

**Level: EMIRATE-WIDE** for DLD transaction fees; federal UAE tax rules apply where relevant.

**Sale registration:** current DLD service pages state **2% seller + 2% buyer** of sale value, plus the published title/map/trustee and knowledge/innovation fees. A private contract can reallocate economic cost between parties, but that does not change the official DLD fee schedule.

**Annual property tax:** Dubai does not operate a Western-style annual ad-valorem property tax on individual ownership. Owners may still face building service charges and other municipal/utility-linked charges; verify the actual development and billing profile.

**Income/capital gains:** the UAE personal-tax environment is structurally different from European systems, but company/business/VAT/corporate-tax questions must be checked separately rather than inferred from the absence of personal income tax.

## E. RESIDENTIAL TENANCIES — RERA-GOVERNED, WITH A BINDING RENT-INCREASE CALCULATOR

**Level: EMIRATE-WIDE** (Law No. 26 of 2007, as amended by Law No. 33 of 2008, and Decree No. 43 of 2013)

**The legal framework:** Dubai's rental market rests on three primary instruments — **Law No. 26 of 2007** ("Regulating the Relationship Between Landlords and Tenants in the Emirate of Dubai"), establishing foundational rights, mandatory Ejari registration, and the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre (RDSC); **Law No. 33 of 2008**, amending rent-increase mechanisms and **extending the eviction notice period from 6 months to 12 months**; and **Decree No. 43 of 2013**, which introduced the current **tiered rent-increase calculator** linked to the RERA Rental Index, replacing the earlier Decree No. 26 of 2011.

**Ejari registration — mandatory and foundational:** every tenancy contract in Dubai **must** be registered through **Ejari** to be legally recognized. **An unregistered contract is not enforceable before the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre.** Ejari registration is also required for DEWA (utility) activation and for most residence-visa processes linked to a tenancy address. Registration costs approximately **AED 195–240** depending on the channel (online via the Dubai REST app, or at an approved service center). A property cannot carry two simultaneous active Ejari registrations — the prior one must be canceled before a new tenant registers.

**Rent increases — governed by a legally binding official calculator, not negotiation:** the **RERA Rental Index / rent increase calculator**, hosted by the DLD, determines the maximum permissible annual rent increase for any residential or commercial property, based on how the current rent compares to the official index for that area and property type. **This tool is not advisory — its result is legally binding.** If the calculator indicates no increase is permitted, the landlord cannot raise the rent regardless of market listing prices; conversely, if an increase is confirmed as permitted within the calculated percentage, the tenant must accept it. The current tiered structure (per Decree No. 43 of 2013) uses a **five-band percentage system (0% / 5% / 10% / 15% / 20%)**, with the maximum 20% increase permitted only where the current rent is more than 40% below the area's market average. **Landlords must provide written notice of any rent increase at least 90 days before the lease expiry date.**

**Eviction — 12 months' notice via notary public for owner's-own-use grounds:** a landlord seeking eviction for personal use (or a specified relative's use) must provide **12 months' written notice**, delivered via **notary public or registered mail**. **If the landlord re-lets the property within two years of such an eviction, the former tenant can claim compensation of up to one year's rent** — a meaningful deterrent against pretextual evictions. Valid eviction grounds also include demolition and substantial renovation of the property.

**Security deposit:** standard practice is **5% of annual rent for an unfurnished property, 10% for a furnished property**.

**Dispute resolution:** the **Rental Dispute Settlement Centre (RDSC)** handles tenancy disputes; the filing fee is **3.5% of the annual rent** (minimum AED 500, maximum AED 20,000). Simple cases are often resolved within **2–4 weeks**; more complex cases may take 1–3 months, with an appeal process adding a further 2–4 weeks if either party appeals.

**Maintenance responsibility:** the landlord is responsible for major maintenance and structural repairs unless the tenancy contract states otherwise.

**Early termination:** governed by the specific tenancy contract; most contracts include an early-termination penalty clause, typically equivalent to two months' rent.

## F. JOINTLY OWNED PROPERTY (OWNERS' ASSOCIATIONS / SERVICE CHARGES)

**Level: EMIRATE-WIDE** (Law No. 27 of 2007 concerning Jointly Owned Property, and related RERA regulations)

**Terminology:** Dubai's equivalent of a condominium/community-of-owners regime governs multi-unit developments and gated communities, with an **Owners' Association (OA)** (or, in practice, often a professional management company acting under RERA's Jointly Owned Property regulations) responsible for administering shared facilities and collecting **service charges**.

**Service charges:** cover building/community maintenance, security, shared facility upkeep (pools, gyms, landscaping), insurance, and management fees — set annually via a budget process and disclosed to owners; **RERA emphasizes clear disclosure of service charges and budgets** for jointly owned properties, and buyers are encouraged to review these statements carefully as part of due diligence before purchase, since service charge levels vary significantly by development and directly affect net investment returns.

**No annual property tax, but service charges are a real and material recurring cost:** given the absence of any annual property tax (Section D), service charges function as Dubai's closest analogue to the recurring ownership costs seen in other jurisdictions in this series, and should be budgeted carefully — they can vary substantially between older and newer developments, and between different building types.

**Buyer's due diligence:** obtain the development's service charge history, current budget, and any special assessment history before purchase — an underfunded or poorly managed jointly-owned development can carry meaningfully higher ongoing costs than the headline service-charge rate might suggest.

## G. FOREIGN BUYER

**Level: EMIRATE-WIDE** for property ownership; residence permits involve Dubai/federal immigration authorities.

**Freehold:** foreign ownership is available in designated Dubai freehold areas under the Dubai framework; verify the specific parcel/project and title type with DLD.

**DLD Golden Visa service:** DLD's live investor service states that an investor owning one or more properties with **purchase value of at least AED 2,000,000** can apply for a **10-year renewable residence permit**. For mortgaged property, DLD requires bank documentation/NOC and its page refers to paid amount/balance conditions.

**Official-authority divergence:** federal ICP public material may describe the real-estate-investor Golden Residency using a different duration/loan formulation from the live DLD Dubai service. This guide **does not silently choose one authority over the other**. The issuing route and current eligibility must be confirmed directly with DLD/ICP before funds are committed for immigration purposes.

**Taskeen — corrected:** the live DLD **2-year Investor Residence (Taskeen)** service does **not** state the older fixed minimum commonly repeated in secondary guides for individual ownership. It states that an **individual property owner may apply regardless of property value**; for **joint ownership**, the applicant's share must be at least **AED 400,000**. The applicant must meet the service's other requirements.

**Off-plan:** do not make a blanket Golden Visa/off-plan eligibility statement unless the live issuing service confirms the applicant's Oqood/title and payment facts.

## H. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

1. **Can a non-resident foreigner buy in Dubai?** Yes in designated freehold areas, subject to DLD project/title checks.
2. **Is the DLD sale-registration fee “4% paid by buyer” as a legal rule?** No. DLD's current schedule states **2% seller + 2% buyer**; a contract may shift the economic burden.
3. **Is there a personal capital-gains tax on an individual's Dubai property sale?** The UAE personal-tax framework does not impose the European-style personal CGT described elsewhere in this series; business/company cases must be checked separately.
4. **Is there annual ad-valorem property tax?** No comparable annual property tax, but service/municipal charges remain.
5. **What does the live DLD Golden Visa investor service state?** AED 2 million property purchase value and a 10-year renewable permit, subject to DLD conditions.
6. **Can I rely on one web page alone for immigration structuring?** No; DLD and federal ICP material must be reconciled with the issuing authority for the applicant's route.
7. **Does Taskeen use the older fixed-value threshold commonly repeated in secondary guides?** No. DLD's live 2-year service says individual owners may apply regardless of property value; joint owners need a share of at least AED 400,000.
8. **Does off-plan automatically qualify?** Do not assume so; confirm the live service's accepted property registration/payment evidence.
9. **Is Ejari relevant to long-term tenancy?** Yes; use the current DLD/RERA service requirements.
10. **Can rent increases be guessed from a generic percentage?** Use the live RERA/DLD rental-index mechanism for the property and notice period.
11. **Does a sale automatically terminate an existing tenancy?** No; tenancy rights/notice must follow applicable Dubai law.
12. **Do holiday homes need Dubai tourism authorisation?** Yes, the DET/DTCM holiday-home framework requires licensing/permit compliance.
13. **Is the legal definition simply “any stay under six months”?** Do not use that secondary-source shortcut; the primary holiday-home resolution defines the regulated accommodation and permit regime without that canonical threshold.
14. **How long is a holiday-home unit permit?** The primary resolution provides a one-year permit, renewable under the regime.
15. **What should be checked before purchase?** DLD title/freehold status, fees, service charges, tenancy, holiday-home eligibility and, if relevant, immigration eligibility with the issuing authority.

## I. GLOSSARY

- **DLD (Dubai Land Department):** the government authority responsible for real estate registration, title deeds, and transfer fees in Dubai.
- **RERA (Real Estate Regulatory Agency):** the regulatory arm of the DLD overseeing the rental market, broker licensing, and jointly owned property regulations.
- **Freehold zone:** a designated area where foreign nationals may hold full ownership title, established under Law No. 7 of 2006 and Regulation No. 3 of 2006.
- **Title deed:** the official DLD-issued ownership document for a completed property purchase.
- **Oqood:** the interim DLD registration certificate for an off-plan property, converted to a full title deed on project completion.
- **MoU/SPA (Memorandum of Understanding / Sales and Purchase Agreement):** the initial and formal contractual documents in a Dubai property purchase.
- **NOC (No Objection Certificate):** a developer- or bank-issued document confirming no outstanding obligations on a property, required before transfer.
- **Ejari:** the mandatory Dubai tenancy contract registration system, a precondition for legal enforceability, utility connection, and most visa processes tied to the address.
- **RDSC (Rental Dispute Settlement Centre):** the body adjudicating Dubai tenancy disputes.
- **RERA Rental Index / calculator:** the official, legally binding tool determining maximum permissible annual rent increases.
- **Jointly Owned Property / Owners' Association (OA):** Dubai's equivalent of a condominium/community-of-owners regime for shared developments.
- **Service charges:** the recurring fees paid by owners in jointly owned developments for maintenance of shared facilities — Dubai's closest analogue to a recurring ownership cost, given the absence of property tax.
- **Golden Visa:** the UAE's principal long-term (10-year) residency-by-investment programme, with a property-investment route from AED 2,000,000.
- **DTCM / DET (Department of Tourism and Commerce Marketing / Department of Economy and Tourism):** the Dubai government body regulating short-term/holiday-home rental, DTCM having been absorbed into DET.
- **Holiday Home Permit:** the mandatory DET/DTCM license required for any short-term rental listing in Dubai.

## SEPARATE MODULE: SHORT-TERM RENTAL (HOLIDAY HOMES)

**Regulator:** Dubai's holiday-home regime is administered under the Dubai tourism authority framework (DET; historically DTCM).

**Primary-rule correction:** the canonical rule is a **licence/permit regime for Holiday Homes**, not a secondary-source duration shortcut such as “under six months” or “under one year”. Administrative Resolution No. 1 of 2020 defines the regulated Holiday Home framework and requires the relevant licence and a permit for the unit.

**Permit duration:** the unit permit is issued for **one year** and is renewable under the applicable rules. Operator/licence validity and unit standards must be verified from the current DET system.

**Property documents/building permission:** title/ownership authority and any developer/building/NOC requirement must be checked for the individual unit in the live application process.

**Fees, Tourism Dirham, VAT and penalties:** use current official DET/DLD schedules; this re-audit does not freeze secondary-source estimates as statutory amounts where a primary schedule was not verified.
