> **Methodological note — produced in English rather than Greek.** Cyprus's official language is Greek (with Turkish also official, though this guide covers the government-controlled area only, administered by the Republic of Cyprus). This guide is produced in English — one of the six languages already used across this series — rather than introducing Greek-Cypriot as a separate production language, since Cyprus follows a **common-law legal system** (inherited from British colonial administration) in which English is extensively used in practice: contracts, title deed documentation, and much professional legal work are routinely conducted in English, and the government's own English-language guidance is authoritative and widely relied upon by both local and international practitioners. Cyprus was selected for this series because it is undergoing **the most comprehensive property tax reform in decades**, effective 1 January 2026, alongside an active, evolving permanent-residence investment route and a maturing short-term-rental registration regime.

## A. BUYING A PROPERTY

**Level: NATIONAL** (Sale of Land (Specific Performance) Law, Department of Lands and Surveys)

**Typical sequence:**
1. **Reservation agreement:** an initial, non-binding step once a property is identified; a reservation deposit is typically forfeited if the buyer withdraws without a legitimate legal reason (such as a title problem discovered during due diligence) — engaging your lawyer **before**, not after, paying any deposit is strongly advised.
2. **Independent legal due diligence:** a Cyprus Bar Association-registered lawyer conducts a Land Search Certificate and verifies clean title, valid building permits, and the absence of undisclosed mortgages or encumbrances — described repeatedly across sources as the single most critical step, given Cyprus's historical (though now much-improved) issues with delayed title deed issuance for new-build developments.
3. **Contract of Sale and deposit:** once due diligence is complete, the buyer signs the Contract of Sale and pays a deposit — typically **20%–30%** of the purchase price.
4. **Deposit at the Land Registry ("Specific Performance"):** your lawyer deposits the signed contract at the District Lands Office, generally within **six months** of signing (some sources cite 60 days as a stricter internal target). This step is legally protective: **the seller cannot resell the property or place new mortgages on it while the contract remains registered** — a mechanism broadly analogous to registering a compromis in France or a preliminary contract in Italy, but distinctively named "Specific Performance" under Cyprus law (Law 81(I)/2011).
5. **Non-EU buyers — Council of Ministers approval:** where required (see Section G), this step must be completed before final transfer.
6. **Title deed transfer:** once all contractual conditions are met (approval where applicable, final payment), the parties attend the Land Registry for the formal transfer of the Title Deed into the buyer's name. **A Title Deed is the official certificate of ownership registered with the Department of Lands and Surveys — without it, you do not legally own the property**, even after paying in full and taking possession.

**Comparison with UK conveyancing:** the overall process is broadly similar to England and Wales — a solicitor is engaged, searches are conducted, contracts are exchanged, and completion follows — reflecting Cyprus's shared common-law heritage.

**Timeline:** typically **2 to 6 months** in total — faster for a new build from a developer with a ready Title Deed, longer for resale properties, and an additional **2–3 months** where Council of Ministers approval is required for a non-EU buyer.

**Legal fees:** typically **€1,000–€3,000** for a standard residential purchase (some sources cite up to 1–1.5% of price for larger transactions) — consistently described as money well spent relative to the cost of a subsequent legal problem.

## B. SELLING A PROPERTY

**Level: NATIONAL**

The seller must have available the registered Title Deed (or contract of sale if title has not yet issued), confirmation of any outstanding mortgage for discharge, and evidence that applicable **municipality, sewerage and other property-specific liabilities** are settled for transfer. Cyprus has **no national annual Immovable Property Tax from 2017 onward** (see Section D).

**Costs:** by market convention, **agent commission falls on the seller** in Cyprus (a point of contrast with several jurisdictions elsewhere in this series where the buyer commonly bears this cost). The seller is also liable for their own Capital Gains Tax exposure on any taxable gain (Section C).

## C. TAXATION OF CAPITAL GAINS — CURRENT OFFICIAL BASELINE / 2026 REFORM CHECK

**Level: NATIONAL** (Capital Gains Tax Law).

The Cyprus Tax Department's current public real-estate guidance confirms the core rule that **Capital Gains Tax is imposed at 20%** on profit from disposal of immovable property in Cyprus (and qualifying disposals of shares in companies holding Cyprus immovable property), irrespective of tax residence, subject to the legislation and double-tax-treaty provisions.

**2026 reform caution:** the Tax Department also maintains a dedicated **Tax Reform 2026** hub, while its public Capital Gains Tax property page still displays older lifetime-exemption figures and older update dates. This re-audit therefore **does not freeze new 2026 lifetime-exemption amounts, indirect-property thresholds or other reform figures unless they are reproduced in current official legislation/guidance available for the transaction date**.

**Indexation / exemptions / non-taxable disposals:** these exist under the statutory regime, but exact amounts, eligibility and any 2026 amendments must be confirmed against the current consolidated legislation and Tax Department implementation before a sale is structured.

## D. TAXES ON ACQUISITION AND ON PROPERTY OWNERSHIP

**Level: NATIONAL**, with local municipal/sewerage charges.

**Stamp duty:** Cyprus Tax Department lists **Law 239(I)/2025 as the repealing law for the Stamp Laws 1963–2025** and published a repeal announcement on 7 January 2026. Therefore new 2026 contracts must not be described using the old ad-valorem stamp-duty schedule as if it remains generally current. Legacy documents/transitional stamping must be handled under the Tax Department's published procedures.

**Transfer fees / VAT:** transfer-fee and VAT treatment depends on whether VAT applies and the nature of the acquisition. The reduced VAT route for a qualifying primary residence and any transfer-fee discount must be checked against the current Department of Lands/Tax Department conditions for the specific transaction.

**Annual ownership tax:** the former national Immovable Property Tax was repealed from 2017; local municipality/sewerage and communal charges remain distinct.

**LEGAL_STATUS: TRANSACTION_SPECIFIC_CONFIRMATION REQUIRED** for VAT/transfer-fee relief and any legacy stamp issue.

## E. RESIDENTIAL TENANCIES

**Level: NATIONAL**, with contractual/court distinctions.

**Electronic rent payment — confirmed:** the Cyprus Tax Department announced that **from 1 July 2026 rent must be paid using electronic means of payment**. This is a current official compliance point and replaces the earlier speculative wording.

**Stamping of new rental agreements:** because the Stamp Laws 1963–2025 were repealed by Law 239(I)/2025, do **not** state that every new 2026 rental agreement must still be stamped within 30 days under the old regime. Legacy/transitional documents must follow Tax Department guidance.

**Rental-income taxation:** older Tax Department pages still display pre-reform income/SDC/GeSY information, while the Tax Department also maintains a dedicated **Tax Reform 2026** hub and 2026 income-tax amendments. For canonical 2026 publication, detailed rates/deductions from stale pages are **not frozen as current** without checking the amended legislation/2026 guidance.

**Courts/tenancy type:** the distinction between statutory/rent-control and ordinary contractual tenancies remains fact-specific; confirm court jurisdiction and termination rights for the property/lease.

## F. MANAGEMENT OF SHARED (COMMUNAL) BUILDINGS

**Level: NATIONAL**, with a **reform bill in progress that, notably, does NOT resolve one of the sector's most persistent problems**

**Terminology:** Cyprus refers to shared building/complex charges as **"Communal Fees"** or **common expenses**, administered by a **management council** (or an owners' committee) responsible for maintaining shared spaces — entrances, lifts, pools, gardens, and increasingly (in newer 2026-era developments) coworking spaces, gyms, and smart irrigation systems.

**What common expenses cover:** the standard 2026 service package typically includes regular cleaning and chemical treatment of swimming pools, landscaping and communal garden maintenance, hallway and parking-area lighting, and elevator and fire-safety system maintenance. Modern complexes increasingly use dedicated apps allowing owners to view fund balances in real time, receive receipts, vote on agendas, and submit repair requests — alongside a growing trend toward energy-saving investments (e.g., solar panels for common-area electricity, which can account for up to 40% of total communal expenses) to offset rising utility costs.

**LEGAL_STATUS: REQUIRES_LOCAL_CONFIRMATION — pending reform bill, with a significant, deliberately-unresolved gap.** Cyprus is progressing a new bill designed to tighten the management and maintenance of jointly-owned buildings from 1 January 2026 onward. **However, the draft law explicitly does NOT address the problem of unpaid communal charges** — under both the current framework and the pending bill, an owner who refuses to pay their proportionate share of common expenses **remains free to continue living in the building, to bequeath it to heirs, and to defer payment indefinitely until the property is eventually sold**. The **only** current means for a management council to recover unpaid communal charges is through the **courts** — described as a costly and time-consuming process, and one the pending reform does not meaningfully improve. **This is a notable structural weakness relative to other jurisdictions in this series** (e.g., Spain's condominio debt attaching automatically to the unit and transferring with sale, or several other jurisdictions' privileged-creditor mechanisms) — buyers of Cyprus apartments in complexes with a history of unpaid communal fees should factor this enforcement gap into their due diligence, since there is no equivalent automatic debt-follows-the-unit mechanism forcing resolution at the point of sale.

**Buyer's due diligence:** review the management council's accounts, the level of common expenses relative to the complex's condition, and — given the enforcement gap described above — specifically investigate the payment history and any long-standing arrears among existing owners, which the current legal framework does little to resolve even after the pending reform.

## G. FOREIGN BUYER

**Level: NATIONAL**

**EU/EEA citizens:** buy with **no restrictions** — the process is identical to that for a Cypriot national.

**Non-EU citizens (including UK nationals since Brexit) — Council of Ministers approval required:** non-EU buyers, including British nationals (who lost their prior EU-equivalent treatment following Brexit), must obtain **permission from the Council of Ministers** before completing a purchase, applied for through the **District Administration Office**. Key features:
- **Approval is virtually guaranteed in practice** — refusals are rare and occur only in unusual circumstances; this is described consistently across sources as a formality rather than a genuine gatekeeping mechanism for ordinary residential purchases.
- **Timeline:** typically **2–3 months**, adding to the overall purchase timeline described in Section A.
- **Scope limitation:** non-EU buyers are **usually capped at one home or a small plot** under this permission route — a structural restriction on the scale of direct non-EU individual ownership, distinct from the EU/EEA position.
- A specific procedural mechanism sometimes referenced is **"COMM 145"**, relevant where Council of Ministers approval interacts with specific transaction structures — confirm current procedural requirements with a Cyprus-qualified lawyer, as this is a technical area subject to periodic administrative updates.

**No automatic residence right from property ownership alone:** simply owning Cyprus property does **not** confer a right to live in Cyprus. Without a residence permit, a non-EU owner can stay only **90 days in any 180-day period** — the same Schengen-adjacent limitation described elsewhere in this series, even though Cyprus is not yet a Schengen member itself.

**Category 6(2) Permanent Residence — the primary investment-linked residence route:** a purchase of **€300,000 or more** can support an application under **Regulation 6(2) ("Category 6.2")**, Cyprus's principal permanent-residence-by-investment route for third-country nationals. Key features:
- **Not a citizenship scheme** — it grants **permanent residence status**, not citizenship; however, where an applicant later resides in Cyprus on a genuine, ongoing basis and separately satisfies statutory naturalisation conditions, that residence history may become relevant to a future citizenship application.
- **The acquisition must generally be a first sale from a development company** (i.e., typically a new-build purchase, not a resale) — the investment may consist of one or two units, provided both are acquired from the same developer and together meet the required threshold.
- **Income requirement:** a minimum annual income of **€50,000** for the main applicant, increased by **€15,000** for a spouse and **€10,000** for each minor child, sourced from outside Cyprus.
- **Clean criminal record** and general suitability requirements apply.
- **LEGAL_STATUS: REQUIRES_LOCAL_CONFIRMATION** — Cyprus is reported to be actively progressing toward **Schengen accession**, with a publicly stated (though unconfirmed) objective often referenced around 2026; if and when this occurs, the practical value of Cyprus permanent residence for European mobility would increase significantly. Confirm the current Schengen accession status at the time of any residence-linked investment decision.

## H. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

1. **Was national Immovable Property Tax reintroduced?** No; the old national tax was repealed from 2017, though local/sewerage/communal charges remain.
2. **Is old stamp duty still generally due on contracts signed in 2026?** Do not assume so; Law 239(I)/2025 repealed the Stamp Laws 1963–2025, with legacy/transitional handling under Tax Department guidance.
3. **Must rent be paid electronically?** Yes — Tax Department announcements state the obligation applies from **1 July 2026**.
4. **Can this guide safely reuse old 2021/2025 rental-tax rate pages as the full 2026 answer?** No; Cyprus has a 2026 tax reform and amended income-tax legislation, so exact current rental-income/SDC/GeSY treatment requires current confirmation.
5. **Is there a national annual property tax?** No national Immovable Property Tax, but local and communal charges apply.
6. **Do non-EU buyers face an approval layer?** Yes, subject to the statutory property-acquisition framework and transaction specifics.
7. **Does property ownership alone guarantee Schengen mobility?** No; Cyprus/Schengen status and permit rights must be verified currently.
8. **Does property automatically grant citizenship?** No.
9. **Is self-service/short-term accommodation registered?** Yes; the Deputy Ministry of Tourism operates an application for Special Label and Registration Number for Self-Service (Sharing Economy) Accommodation Establishments.
10. **Can I list first and register later?** Do not assume so; verify the current registration/label requirements before listing.
11. **Do communal-building rules still matter?** Yes, independently from tourism registration.
12. **Is a Title Deed critical?** Yes; title status and any delayed-title issues require independent due diligence.
13. **Does VAT apply to every property purchase?** No; treatment depends on new/resale/primary-residence and other statutory conditions.
14. **Can transfer fees coexist with VAT on the same basis?** The acquisition structure must be checked; do not double-count headline rates mechanically.
15. **What should be checked before an investment?** Title, buyer-approval status, VAT/fees, 2026 rental-tax rules, electronic rent payments and tourism registration.

## I. GLOSSARY

- **Title Deed:** the official Department of Lands and Surveys certificate of ownership; without it, ownership is not legally complete.
- **Specific Performance (Law 81(I)/2011):** the mechanism by which depositing a signed contract at the Land Registry protects a buyer against the seller reselling or re-mortgaging the property.
- **Land Search Certificate:** the pre-purchase due-diligence document confirming clean title and disclosing encumbrances.
- **Transfer fees:** the tiered Department of Lands and Surveys charge paid on Title Deed transfer, waived where VAT applies, and discounted 50% on most resales.
- **Council of Ministers approval:** the near-automatic permission process required for non-EU buyers before completing a purchase.
- **Category 6.2 (Regulation 6(2)):** Cyprus's principal permanent-residence-by-investment route, requiring a EUR 300,000+ first-sale developer purchase.
- **Capital Gains Tax (CGT):** the flat 20% tax on gains from disposing of Cyprus immovable property, with materially increased 2026 lifetime exemptions (EUR 30,000 general / EUR 150,000 primary residence / EUR 50,000 agricultural).
- **Indexation allowance:** the inflation adjustment applied to original purchase price when calculating a taxable capital gain.
- **Communal Fees / common expenses:** the mandatory charges for maintaining shared areas of a building or complex, managed by a management council.
- **Rent Control Court / District Court:** the two distinct courts handling Cyprus tenancy disputes, according to whether the tenancy is a "statutory tenancy" or an ordinary contractual one.
- **GHS/GESY contribution:** the 2.65% General Healthcare System levy on gross rental income.
- **Special Defence Contribution (SDC):** the 3% levy on 75% of rental income, applicable specifically to Cyprus-domiciled individuals.
- **Register of Self-Catering Accommodation:** the mandatory Deputy Ministry of Tourism register for any short-term/holiday letting under Law 34(I)/2019.

## SEPARATE MODULE: SHORT-TERM / SELF-CATERING RENTAL

**Official regime:** Cyprus uses a national registration framework for **Self-Service (Sharing Economy) Accommodation Establishments**. The Deputy Ministry of Tourism provides an official application for the **Special Label and Registration Number**.

**Canonical consequence:** it is incorrect to describe Cyprus short-term letting as an unregistered grey area. An operator must verify eligibility, obtain/maintain the required registration/label and display/use the number as required by the current regime.

**Tax:** short-term/self-service income has separate tax/VAT compliance implications. Because 2026 tax reform changed the wider tax framework and some Tax Department web pages remain pre-reform, exact 2026 income-tax/SDC/GeSY/VAT treatment must be confirmed from current legislation/guidance for the operator.

**Building layer:** tourism registration does not override title, jointly-owned-building rules, management council decisions or contractual restrictions.
