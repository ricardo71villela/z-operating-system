## A. BUYING A PROPERTY

**Level: NATIONAL** (Civil Code), with minor **MUNICIPAL** variation in administrative practice.

**Typical sequence:**
1. **Reservation or preliminary agreement:** an initial document, often with a deposit, preceding the notarial deed.
2. **Title verification:** checking the land and mortgage register (księga wieczysta — an electronic register maintained by district courts, searchable online at ekw.ms.gov.pl), the seller's basis of ownership, any encumbrances (mortgages, easements), and — for a unit in a multi-unit building — the owners' association or housing cooperative documents.
3. **Notarial deed (akt notarialny):** a **mandatory formal requirement** for transferring property ownership in Poland. The notary, as an independent public official, confirms the parties' identities, ensures they understand the contract and act voluntarily, and checks the land register status and any encumbrances. **The notarial deed is what actually transfers ownership.**
4. **Registration in the Land and Mortgage Register (KW):** after signing, the notary files an application with the competent land register court to register the new owner (and any mortgage, if applicable). Court fees: 200 PLN for ownership registration plus another 200 PLN for mortgage registration.
5. **Property tax registration:** the new owner should notify the municipal (gmina) office within **14 days** to avoid the tax being assessed late with penalty interest.

**Notarial costs:** maximum rates are regulated by the Ministry of Justice regulation of 28 June 2004 — the notary cannot charge more, though may charge less; the fee is calculated on a progressive scale based on property value, plus 23% VAT on the notary's fee. Example for an 800,000 PLN apartment: notarial fee approximately 3,970 PLN + VAT = approximately 4,883 PLN.

**Primary vs. secondary market — an important tax distinction:** buying from a developer on the **primary market** attracts **VAT** (23%, or 8% for housing under a specific affordable-housing programme, already embedded in the price) — **PCC does not apply**. Buying on the **secondary market** (from a private individual) attracts **PCC (tax on civil-law transactions) at 2%** of the market value, payable by the buyer — see Section D.

## B. SELLING A PROPERTY

**Level: NATIONAL**

The seller should prepare: an up-to-date land register extract, proof of title, arrears clearance certificates (property tax, association/cooperative fees), and — where a mortgage is being repaid — a bank confirmation for mortgage discharge.

**Price verification by the tax authority:** if the sale price deviates significantly from market value, the tax authority may challenge the transaction and require an expert valuation, treating the market value as the basis for tax purposes — an important warning for transactions involving an artificially low declared price.

**Typical seller costs:** agency commission (if applicable, typically 1.5%–3% net) and the seller's own income tax exposure on any gain (Section C). PCC is always paid by the buyer, never the seller.

## C. TAXATION OF GAINS ON SALE

**Level: NATIONAL** (PIT Act).

**Five-year rule:** for a private individual, disposal before the end of the **fifth calendar year counted from the end of the year of acquisition/construction** is within the PIT real-estate disposal regime. A disposal after that period is outside PIT on this basis.

**Inheritance:** for inherited property, the five-year period is counted from the end of the calendar year in which the **decedent** acquired or constructed the property. Do **not** extend this decedent rule to ordinary gifts without separate statutory verification.

**PIT-39 filing:** the Ministry of Finance states that the return is filed where the disposal occurs within the five-year period, regardless of whether it produced income or loss. **After the five-year period, no PIT return is filed for that disposal.**

**Tax rate / housing relief:** taxable gain within the period is generally subject to the statutory 19% regime, with housing-purpose relief potentially available where statutory expenditure and timing conditions are met. The exact catalogue of qualifying expenditure must be checked against current Ministry guidance before relying on the relief.

## D. TAXES ON ACQUISITION AND ON PROPERTY OWNERSHIP

**Level: NATIONAL** (PCC, VAT) / **MUNICIPAL** (annual property tax)

**At acquisition — PCC (tax on civil-law transactions):** **2%** of the market value of the property on the secondary market, payable by the buyer, collected directly by the notary at the deed and remitted to the tax office. **The primary market (purchase from a developer) is exempt from PCC** — VAT applies instead, embedded in the price.

**PCC first-home exemption:** the statutory exemption is based on the purchaser being a natural person (or qualifying co-purchasers) who has not previously held the specified residential ownership/cooperative rights, subject to the statutory inherited-share exception. **It is not an “under-35” exemption.** Nationality/tax-residence eligibility should be checked against the exact transaction facts rather than inferred from an age rule.

**Annual property tax — municipal:** maximum rates are set centrally each year (indexed to inflation), but **each municipality (gmina) sets its own rate** within these caps — there is no single nationwide rate. The tax is generally modest by European standards (one source cites a figure of roughly 63 PLN for the first year on a typical apartment).

**Never automatically equate** Polish PCC with Spanish ITP, French DMTO, or Italian imposta di registro — the 2% rate is lower than in many other jurisdictions in this series, and the primary-market exemption (VAT embedded in price instead of PCC) is a distinctive feature of the Polish system.

**No national wealth tax:** Poland has no current equivalent to Spain's Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio or France's IFI — the only recurring annual charge is the modest, municipally-set property tax.

**Private rental income — flat-rate taxation:** since 2023, private rental by individuals is taxed **exclusively via flat-rate taxation on registered revenue (ryczałt)** — **8.5%** of revenue up to 100,000 PLN per year and **12.5%** on the excess (spouses can pool their threshold to 200,000 PLN by filing a joint declaration). There is no longer an option to tax private rental under general rules with cost deductions.

**VAT registration threshold:** for business activity related to real estate (e.g., larger-scale rental), the VAT exemption threshold rose to **240,000 PLN** annual turnover in 2026.

## E. RESIDENTIAL TENANCIES

**Level: NATIONAL** (Civil Code and the Act on the Protection of Tenants' Rights, the Housing Stock of the Municipality, and the Amendment of the Civil Code)

**Two main tenancy models in Poland:**
- **Standard lease:** subject to full statutory tenant protection under the tenant-protection act — landlord termination is limited to specific statutory grounds (e.g., rent arrears above a defined threshold, use of the premises contrary to the contract, need to demolish or renovate the building), with appropriate notice periods. Eviction of a tenant who does not vacate voluntarily requires a court judgment, and the tenant may be entitled to social housing in certain circumstances — a relatively lengthy process protective of the tenant.
- **Occasional lease (najem okazjonalny):** a form of lease specifically designed to help landlords recover their property more easily, requiring a **notarial deed** containing the tenant's declaration submitting to enforcement and undertaking to vacate the premises by a specified date, together with the designation of an **alternative dwelling** the tenant could move to if eviction becomes necessary. The agreement must be reported to the relevant tax office within 14 days of signing. This form is considerably more popular among market landlords, precisely because of its simplified and faster repossession procedure compared with the standard lease.

**Deposit:** market practice, usually one to several months' rent; for an occasional lease, the law caps the deposit at a maximum of **six times** the monthly rent.

**Tax office registration/notification:** mandatory for an occasional lease within 14 days; not a condition of validity for a standard lease, but rental income is always taxable via the flat rate described in Section D, regardless of the lease form or its notification.

## F. OWNERS' ASSOCIATIONS — LEGISLATION IN ACTIVE REFORM

**Level: NATIONAL** (Act on Ownership of Premises), **undergoing a significant amendment reportedly targeted for 2026**

**Bodies:** an **owners' association (wspólnota mieszkaniowa)** arises automatically by operation of law once the first unit in a building is separated into individual ownership — it comprises all unit owners collectively. It is managed by a **board (zarząd)** (elected by the owners) or an external manager acting under a management agreement. Key decisions are taken by **resolutions of the unit owners**, voted proportionally to their share in the common property (generally corresponding to unit floor area), at a meeting, by individual vote collection, or in a mixed procedure.

**Renovation fund (fundusz remontowy):** separate from the day-to-day operating fund, intended for the long-term accumulation of funds for renovation and modernisation expenditure — established by a resolution of the owners.

**LEGAL_STATUS: REQUIRES_LOCAL_CONFIRMATION — a 2026 amendment to the Act on Ownership of Premises.** According to sources consulted for this guide, the proposed changes reportedly include:
- **Mandatory electronic financial reporting** for associations with more than 7 units (an online annual financial report, an online-accessible register of resolutions, digital meeting minutes).
- A requirement for **managers and administrators to hold liability insurance** and to maintain documentation digitally.
- A **statutory right for unit owners to inspect** the association's documents electronically.
- **Formal definition of the renovation fund** as separate from the operating fund, with a specific requirement that funds accumulated in it may only be spent on maintaining the property in proper technical condition (not on day-to-day operating costs).
- A possible requirement to prepare **multi-year renovation plans**, based on periodic technical building inspections, approved by resolution — intended to reduce sudden, large fee increases.
- New **quorum requirements** for resolutions adopted by individual vote collection and mixed procedures, with a minimum (two-week) and maximum period for casting votes, set by each association individually.

**This package of changes had not been fully enacted as of this guide's research date** — the exact legislative status and implementation date should be verified directly before any transaction.

**Short-term rental and the owners' association — a key point, see the separate module below:** the owners' association currently has very limited ability to block short-term (Airbnb-style) letting of a unit belonging to one of the owners — Polish courts and the Supreme Court have held that a blanket ban imposed by resolution violates the constitutional right to property, though **this may soon change** — see the separate module.

## G. FOREIGN BUYER

**Level: NATIONAL** (Act on Acquisition of Real Estate by Foreigners; MSWiA).

**EEA and Switzerland:** MSWiA states that nationals/entrepreneurs from the **European Economic Area and the Swiss Confederation do not require the ministerial permit** for acquisition covered by the Act. The final transitional restriction for EU acquisition ended on **1 May 2016**; there was no 2024 “removal” of the general EU/EEA restriction.

**Other foreign buyers:** as a rule, non-EEA/non-Swiss foreign buyers may require an MSWiA permit unless a statutory exemption applies (for example, certain independent residential premises outside the relevant exclusions). Agricultural land and border-area transactions carry additional layers and must be checked separately.

**2026 electronic-transmission reform:** government work in 2026 on electronic transmission of notarial acquisition data to MSWiA is **procedural legislation in development unless and until enacted and effective**. Do not present a government-adopted draft as already-operative law.

**No property Golden Visa:** purchase of ordinary residential real estate does not by itself create a Polish residence right.

**LEGAL_STATUS: REQUIRES_TRANSACTION_SPECIFIC_CONFIRMATION** — verify purchaser nationality/status, property type, agricultural/border status and statutory exemption with MSWiA/notary before signing.

## H. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

1. **Is a notarial deed required to transfer ownership?** Yes, transfer of Polish real estate requires the prescribed notarial form.
2. **When is a private sale taxed under PIT?** Generally when sold before the end of five calendar years counted from the end of the acquisition/construction year.
3. **What if I sell after five years?** The official Ministry guidance states no PIT is due on that basis and no PIT return is filed for the disposal.
4. **How is inherited property counted?** From the end of the year in which the decedent acquired or constructed it.
5. **Is the PCC first-home exemption an “under-35” relief?** No. The statutory test concerns prior qualifying residential rights (with its statutory exceptions), not a universal age threshold.
6. **Is secondary-market PCC generally 2%?** Yes, subject to statutory exemptions and transaction classification.
7. **Is annual property tax national?** The framework is national but the applicable local rate is municipal within statutory limits.
8. **Do EEA/Swiss buyers need an MSWiA permit?** Generally no under the Act; MSWiA confirms the exemption.
9. **Did this freedom begin in 2024?** No. The final EU transitional restriction ended on 1 May 2016.
10. **Do other foreign buyers always need a permit?** Not always; statutory exemptions exist, but property type/location matter.
11. **Does buying property grant residence?** No, not by itself.
12. **Is there a fully operational new 2026 national Airbnb registry already?** Do not assume so; 2026 legislation is still in the legislative process unless later official enactment is verified.
13. **What is CWTON?** The proposed central register in the 2026 short-term-rental legislative package; treat it as proposed until operative law is confirmed.
14. **Can an owners' association automatically rely on a 2026 draft to demand consent?** No; a draft has no operative force until enacted.
15. **What should an investor verify immediately before a short-term rental purchase?** Current statute/bill status, municipality rules, building decisions, construction-use classification and tax treatment.

## I. GLOSSARY

- **Akt notarialny (notarial deed):** the document drafted by a notary that transfers ownership of real estate.
- **Księga wieczysta (land and mortgage register):** the public register maintained by district courts, recording a property and all rights connected to it.
- **PCC (tax on civil-law transactions):** the 2% tax paid by the buyer on secondary-market purchases.
- **PIT-39:** the declaration used for an individual private real-estate disposal that falls within the statutory five-year PIT period; Ministry guidance states no PIT return is filed for a disposal after that period.
- **Ulga mieszkaniowa (housing relief):** the income-tax exemption available when sale proceeds are used for the seller's own housing needs.
- **Ryczałt (flat-rate taxation):** the mandatory form of taxation for private rental income (8.5%/12.5%).
- **Wspólnota mieszkaniowa (owners' association):** the collective body of all unit owners in a multi-unit building, arising automatically by law.
- **Fundusz remontowy (renovation fund):** the association's fund for long-term renovation expenditure, distinct from the operating fund.
- **Najem okazjonalny (occasional lease):** a lease form involving a notarial submission-to-enforcement deed, facilitating faster repossession by the landlord.
- **MSWiA:** the Ministry of Interior and Administration, the authority issuing permits for property acquisition by non-EEA foreigners.
- **EEA:** the European Economic Area (EU + Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein) — citizens of these states, and of Switzerland, do not need an MSWiA permit.
- **CWTON (or equivalent):** a proposed central multi-sector register of accommodation facilities — the planned short-term rental registration system, not yet enacted as of this guide.
