## A. BUYING A PROPERTY

### A1. Purchase agreement and statutory cooling-off period

For a qualifying residential purchase, after the buyer receives the signed purchase agreement, a statutory **3-day cooling-off period** applies. At least two of those days must not be a Saturday, Sunday or recognised public holiday. The rule is not universal for every possible acquisition: for example, bare land is outside this specific statutory home-purchase cooling-off rule.

**LEGAL_STATUS:** PRIMARY_OFFICIAL — Rijksoverheid.

### A2. Notarial transfer and Kadaster

The civil-law notary prepares the transfer deed (*akte van levering*). Legal ownership is transferred through the notarial deed together with its registration in the **Kadaster** public registers. A signed purchase agreement is therefore not the land-register transfer itself.

**LEGAL_STATUS:** PRIMARY_OFFICIAL — Kadaster.

### A3. Transfer tax (*overdrachtsbelasting*) — 2026

For 2026 the principal official rates relevant to property are:

- **2%** for a home the buyer will occupy as their main residence, when the starter exemption does not apply;
- **8%** for a dwelling the buyer will not use as their main residence, including a second home, holiday home or buy-to-let dwelling;
- **10.4%** for other real estate such as commercial property and undeveloped land.

### A4. Starter exemption — correction of the earlier draft

The one-time **0% startersvrijstelling** may apply when the buyer:

- is at least 18 and **under 35** at acquisition;
- will live in the home themselves for a longer period;
- has **not used the exemption before**; and
- acquires a home whose full market value does not exceed **€555,000 in 2026**.

**Important correction:** the property does **not** have to be the buyer's first-ever home. Prior home ownership by itself does not disqualify the buyer. Eligibility is assessed per buyer, while the €555,000 ceiling is tested against the value of the whole property.

### A5. Acquisition within six months of the seller's acquisition — correction

This is **not a blanket exemption** from transfer tax. Under the statutory anti-double-tax mechanism, the taxable base can be reduced by the amount on which tax was due on the preceding acquisition when the official conditions are met. The exact result depends on the previous acquisition and tax treatment.

**LEGAL_STATUS:** PRIMARY_OFFICIAL — Belastingdienst.

## B. TAXATION OF OWNERSHIP AND SALE

### B1. Owner-occupied home

The Dutch system should not be presented as imposing a conventional standalone capital-gains tax on the sale of an ordinary owner-occupied home. Tax consequences of home ownership and replacement-home equity belong primarily to the **Box 1 / own-home** framework rather than to a sale-triggered CGT model.

### B2. Second homes and investment property — Box 3

Second homes and privately held investment property can fall within **Box 3**. For the **provisional 2026 assessment**, Belastingdienst uses the following notional-return percentages:

- bank balances: **1.28%**;
- investments and other assets: **6.00%**;
- qualifying debts: **2.70%**.

The Box 3 tax rate is **36%** and the 2026 tax-free allowance is **€59,357 per person** (**€118,714 for fiscal partners**).

These figures must not be described as an unavoidable final 6.00%-return tax in every case. Under the statutory **actual-return / counterevidence regime**, a taxpayer may be able to use a lower actual return when the legal conditions are met. Actual return is assessed across the Box 3 portfolio under the applicable rules and may include income and value movements.

### B3. Own use of a Box 3 property from 2026 — correction

Article 5.27a Wet IB 2001 values the benefit of a Box 3 property being available for the taxpayer's own use by reference to its **economic rental value** for the relevant period. The statute does **not** prescribe a blanket fixed percentage of WOZ value for this benefit.

### B4. 2028 reform status — correction

The proposed **Wet werkelijk rendement box 3** was passed by the **Tweede Kamer on 12 February 2026**. The Government continues to target **1 January 2028**, but the **Eerste Kamer still had to consider the bill** at this re-audit date. The earlier draft's statement that the March 2026 Tweede Kamer deadline was still unresolved is obsolete.

**LEGAL_STATUS:** PRIMARY_OFFICIAL current status, but future legislation remains subject to parliamentary change.

## C. ANNUAL LOCAL PROPERTY TAX

Homeowners pay municipal **OZB (onroerendezaakbelasting)**. The amount depends on the municipality's annual OZB rate and the property's **WOZ value**. There is no single national OZB rate.

**LEGAL_STATUS:** PRIMARY_OFFICIAL — Rijksoverheid; local amount requires municipal confirmation.

## D. RESIDENTIAL TENANCIES

### D1. Indefinite contracts are the norm

Since **1 July 2024**, new residential tenants in principle receive an **indefinite-term contract**. Temporary contracts are allowed only in specified statutory situations and are generally capped at **2 years** in those exceptions.

**Important correction:** the earlier general description of 2-year independent-housing and 5-year room contracts as the current default is obsolete. That was the pre-1-July-2024 model.

### D2. WWS / middle-rent regulation — 2026

The **Wet betaalbare huur** extended binding rent-price regulation into the middle-rent segment. In 2026:

- the regulated middle segment runs through **186 WWS points**;
- the 2026 upper middle-rent / liberalisation threshold is **€1,228.07 per month**;
- the 2026 lower middle-rent boundary cited by Rijksoverheid is **€932.93 per month** for contracts beginning in 2026.

Contract date, initial rent and WWS points all matter. Contracts signed before **1 July 2024** can fall under different transitional rules; they must not be treated as one uniform category.

### D3. Rent review / Huurcommissie

Rent-price review rights depend on the segment and contract date. Social and middle-rent tenants have broad statutory price protection; a free-sector tenant generally has a shorter initial review window. This guide therefore does not state an unlimited Huurcommissie review right for every tenancy.

**LEGAL_STATUS:** PRIMARY_OFFICIAL — Rijksoverheid; case-specific review still required.

## E. APARTMENTS AND VvE

A buyer of an apartment right automatically becomes a member of the **Vereniging van Eigenaars (VvE)**. The VvE manages and maintains the common parts of the building.

VvEs must reserve annually for major maintenance. The statutory framework permits calculation by a multi-year maintenance plan (**MJOP**) or, where applicable, at least **0.5% of the building's reconstruction value**, subject to the statutory alternatives.

Before acquisition, a buyer should review the division deed/regulations, meeting decisions, maintenance plan, reserve position and any VvE financing.

**LEGAL_STATUS:** PRIMARY_OFFICIAL — Rijksoverheid.

## F. FOREIGN / NON-RESIDENT BUYER

Property purchase and immigration status are distinct questions. Official Dutch services explicitly contemplate buying or renting a home when relocating to the Netherlands, while residence rights depend separately on nationality and immigration law. A non-resident may also need Dutch administrative identifiers such as a **BSN** obtained through the RNI process depending on their situation.

This foundation does **not** publish a blanket statement that every foreign buyer can acquire every category of Dutch real estate without restriction. Transaction-specific restrictions, municipal rules, sanctions regimes, agricultural/strategic land rules, entity-ownership issues and compliance checks must be confirmed with the notary where relevant.

**LEGAL_STATUS:** REQUIRES_TRANSACTION_SPECIFIC_NOTARY_CONFIRMATION.

## G. SHORT-TERM / TOURIST LETTING — AMSTERDAM REFERENCE MODEL

Tourist letting is highly local. The Amsterdam rules are a reference example and must not be projected automatically onto another municipality.

### G1. General Amsterdam rules

For Amsterdam holiday rental of a principal residence, the municipality requires a permit and advance notification of each rental period. The general city maximum is **30 nights per calendar year** and normally **4 guests** at a time.

### G2. Fifteen-night areas from 1 April 2026 — corrected geography

From **1 April 2026**, a **15-night annual maximum** applies specifically to:

- Haarlemmerbuurt;
- Jordaan;
- Grachtengordel-West;
- Grachtengordel-Zuid;
- Weteringschans;
- Burgwallen-Nieuwe Zijde;
- Nieuwmarkt/Lastage;
- Oude Pijp.

**Important correction:** do not describe the whole Centrum district or all of Nieuwe Pijp as automatically subject to the 15-night limit.

For every other Dutch municipality, verify the municipal registration, permit, night-cap, occupancy, zoning and enforcement rules directly.

**LEGAL_STATUS:** PRIMARY_OFFICIAL for Amsterdam; LOCAL_CONFIRMATION required elsewhere.
